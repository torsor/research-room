# <PROJECT TITLE>

This folder supports an interactive investigation of <RESEARCH TOPIC> with the
user. Its purpose is to increase understanding and preserve useful evidence
while keeping the user able to steer the mathematics.

Read `conventions.md` first, then the relevant file in `goals/` and only the
artifacts needed for the current question. Read a method file when its subject

## Research purpose

A sharper question, a useful example, a computed obstruction, a corrected
conjecture, or a well-documented dead end can be as valuable as a theorem.
Choose work for the understanding it may produce. Do not silently convert an
exploratory exchange into a mandate to finish a theorem or exhaust an approach.

## Work with the user

- Treat a direct instruction to start or proceed as authorization for that
  work. Do not initiate a proposal panel in its place.
- Do not require a proposal for a small example, source check, toy computation,
  or bounded exploration. Proposal panels are for significant new directions
  when requested or otherwise called for by `method/proposals.md`.
- Match the scale and mode of the request: exploration, computation, drafting,
  cleaning, review, or decision.
- Ask when a choice would materially select the mathematical direction or alter
  the intended claim. Continue independent useful work where possible.
- Share provisional thinking early enough for the user to redirect it.
- If the user requests process discussion, discuss the process. Do not silently
  resume mathematical work.
- Respect active collaborators, file ownership, and declared read-only areas.

## Evidence and confidence

Distinguish proposed arguments, finite computations, imported theorems, cleaned
exposition, and independently reviewed arguments. State the assumptions and
range that matter. A dependent conclusion cannot be more secure than the input
on which it relies.

Correct mistakes promptly and inspect affected dependencies. A gap in one
argument does not by itself show that its conclusion is false, and disagreement
about exposition is not a mathematical refutation.

Use the formal status ledger only when the user requests a progress report,
when recording a session closeout, or when mathematical status has materially
changed. Do not append it to ordinary discussion, planning, or process analysis.
See `method/reporting.md` and `method/review.md`.

## Records

- Journal: append useful reasoning, surprises, and corrections.
- Notes: preserve reusable evidence and informative unsuccessful approaches;
  mark supersession rather than erasing substantive history.
- Canonical: maintain concise load-bearing claims with their support; archive
  withdrawn entries and reconsider dependents.
- Goals: keep current questions concise and editable.
- Review: preserve the exact unit assessed, reports, focused responses, and
  outcome without copying the whole history into every summary.
- Optional handoff: maintain one short operational reminder only when it makes
  interrupted work easier to resume. Follow `method/handoffs.md`.

Keep obsolete directions out of current task files. Do not infer replacement,
deactivation, misconduct, or authorization from an old handoff or harsh prose.
The room's community and research values are described in `method/culture.md`.
