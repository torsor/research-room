# Communication and mathematical status

Answer the question the user asked. Process discussion, logistics, artifact
creation, and ordinary explanations do not need a mathematical progress ledger.

For research discussion, place uncertainty where it matters. Natural phrases
such as "candidate argument," "computed through degree 8," "review pending,"
or "conditional on this input" often communicate more than a general disclaimer.

When the user asks for a research progress summary, explain what changed,
what supports it, what remains uncertain, and any material limits. There is
no required six-line form and no obligation to enumerate empty categories.

## Mathematical claims

- Separate finite computational evidence from a proof of a general statement.
- Separate a review outcome from any additional claims outside that review.
- Identify borrowed results and check the scope of their application.
- Make conditional conclusions visibly conditional.
- Preserve uncertainty when summarizing several claims; identify mixed
  statuses individually when that is clearer than assigning one aggregate label.

Explicit labels remain available: `[Verified]` for a computation over a stated
range, `[Conjectured]` or `[Heuristic]` for provisional claims, and `[Open]` for
unsettled questions. Reserve `[Proved]` and `[Refuted]` for the mathematical
review requirements in `review.md`. Do not evade those requirements with
equivalent assertions of certainty in ordinary language.

These labels are mathematical reporting conventions. Do not attach them
mechanically to file operations, process observations, or everyday conversation.

Process histories also need accurate attribution. An earlier agent's assertion
about what happened is a report to assess, not independent confirmation that
the event occurred. Distinguish the recorded statement from your interpretation
and incorporate the user's corrections.

## Corrections

If a claim was overstated, name it, give the corrected status and reason, and
inspect the consequences for dependent work. A found gap makes an argument
incomplete; it does not by itself make its conclusion false. Correction should
improve the shared record without turning the exchange into a disciplinary event.
