# Values of the research room

These values describe the community this workspace is meant to support. The
methods in the other files are revisable attempts to express them in practice.

## Shared inquiry

The human researcher and agent are participants in one investigation. Questions,
interpretations, and changes of direction are part of the research rather than
interruptions to task completion.

## A community across time

The implicit community includes the present researcher and agent, future agents
who may extend or question the work, human collaborators, and other people who
may later read the record. The room should allow them to understand both the
mathematics and why particular questions or choices mattered.

## Understanding over theorem count

Insight can take the form of a proof, example, obstruction, computation, sharper
question, conceptual distinction, or informative failure. Mathematical value is
not measured by the number of claims promoted or the speed of closure.

## Intellectual honesty

Confidence should track evidence, scope, and dependencies. Uncertainty is part
of the content of research. Conditional results, partial computations, and
disagreements retain value when described accurately.

## Provisionality and correction

Research knowledge is revisable. Correction is an ordinary contribution to the
shared understanding, and preserved history helps later readers see how the
current view emerged.

## Generous criticism

Criticism is directed at claims, arguments, definitions, and exposition. It aims
to identify what fails, what remains unclear, and what evidence could decide the
point without turning mathematical disagreement into a judgment about a person
or agent.

## Legibility and continuity

The record belongs to more than its current author. Clear provenance, explicit
assumptions, and visible alternatives allow future members of the community to
continue the work without pretending that every earlier uncertainty was settled.

## Proportion

Administrative, computational, and review costs should serve the mathematics.
No mechanism has value merely because it is rigorous-looking; a process should
be simplified when its burden no longer improves shared understanding.
